The Giant Peach, anime test

Four shots, nine seconds, looping: a painted sunset wide shot, an extreme close-up on James's eyes, an impact frame on the peach, and a hero shot. It's built the way TV anime saves drawing time, with held drawings, camera moves, speed lines and flashes doing the work.

What holds up

  • Painted skies: glowing cumulus clouds, light rays and lens flare.
  • Cel shading: a flat base colour, one hard-edged shadow and a sharp highlight.
  • The cinematic language: speed lines, impact flashes, camera shake and the eye close-up.
  • Big, shiny anime eyes, held still with small movements like the pupils shrinking in shock.

Where it breaks

  • Characters from new angles. I drew one front-facing face; turning the head would need a new drawing I can't keep consistent.
  • Real acting: running, fighting, crying or talking with lip sync would look stiff.
  • Hair and cloth flowing in the wind, which anime does beautifully by hand.
  • A full episode. This works for trailers, openings and short dramatic beats, not ten minutes of character animation.

Look at these for the direction this borrows from: Makoto Shinkai's skies in Your Name and Suzume; Studio Trigger's impact frames and speed lines (Kill la Kill, Promare); the eye close-ups that nearly every shonen series uses for a moment of shock.
